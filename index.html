About

I treat individual variation in cognition as an object of study rather than as measurement noise, using handedness as an exemplar quantifiable biological variable, with hemispheric lateralization and structural brain asymmetry as the measurement handle and visuospatial attention and working memory as the target functions. The translational endpoint is neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric heterogeneity, where atypical lateralization, left-handedness and attentional and working-memory deficits are disproportionately represented.

I hold a Master of Science (by Research) in Cognitive Science from IIT Kanpur, where I worked with Dr. Ark Verma, and an integrated BS-MS in Biology from IISER Mohali, where my master's thesis on comparative genomics was supervised by Dr. Kuljeet Singh Sandhu. In 2024 I was a Talent+ research intern in the Imaging Genomics group at the Max Planck Institute for Psycholinguistics, Nijmegen. Since February 2026 I have been working independently, in collaboration with Dr. Verma, on a three-paper programme and a confirmatory preregistration on handedness and the lateralization of visuospatial attention. I am currently seeking a PhD position.

Research

Handedness and the lateralization of visuospatial attention

Independent researcher, with Dr. Ark Verma (IIT Kanpur) · Feb. 2026 – present

Extending the master's thesis paradigm to a larger sample of 104 participants (102 analysed) recruited across IIT Kanpur and NIT Calicut, and reported preliminarily at CogSci 2023, this work carries the analysis through to completion: two empirical papers, one theoretical framework paper and a confirmatory preregistration, all ready for submission. Hierarchical Bayesian drift-diffusion modelling of 23,273 trials (brms/Stan) localises the visual-field × handedness interaction to nondecision time, with robustness checked by permutation, specification-curve, equivalence and cross-site heterogeneity analyses.

Disorder-associated brain structural asymmetry

Talent+ Research Intern, Max Planck Institute for Psycholinguistics, Nijmegen · Sep. 2024 – Dec. 2024 · Supervisors: Dr. Clyde Francks and Dr. Meng-Yun Wang

Applied multivariate statistics, including principal component analysis and canonical correlation analysis, to disorder-associated measures of brain structural asymmetry, with cortical and subcortical measures extracted from structural MRI using FreeSurfer. Presented as a seminar talk at the Imaging Genomics Group Meeting.

Handedness continuum and lateralized cognitive functions

Master's thesis, IIT Kanpur · May 2019 – Aug. 2021 · Supervisor: Dr. Ark Verma

Four behavioural experiments using the divided visual half-field paradigm, with 39 participants sampled across the full handedness continuum, tested whether the handedness continuum predicts lateralized visual attention. The degree of handedness modulated the degree and direction of lateralization in global–local processing and in face processing, but not in orientation or spatial-frequency processing, identifying low-level visual features as a potential confound in the lateralization literature.

Genomic rearrangement and cancer resistance in rodents

BS-MS thesis, IISER Mohali · May 2017 – Jun. 2018 · Supervisor: Dr. Kuljeet Singh Sandhu

Comparative genomic analysis across mammals uncovered a large rearrangement in the naked mole-rat genome that splits a tumour-suppressor gene cluster relative to humans. The convergent rearrangement is confined to two rodent suborders that also show reduced oncogenesis, bearing on the exceptional cancer resistance of those species.
