About

I treat individual variation in cognition as an object of study rather than as measurement noise, using handedness as an exemplar quantifiable biological variable, with hemispheric lateralization and structural brain asymmetry as the measurement handle and visuospatial attention and working memory as the target functions. The translational endpoint is neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric heterogeneity, where atypical lateralization, left-handedness and attentional and working-memory deficits are disproportionately represented.

I hold a Master of Science (by Research) in Cognitive Science from IIT Kanpur, where I worked with Dr. Ark Verma in the Language and Cognition Lab, and an integrated BS-MS in Biology from IISER Mohali, where my master's thesis on comparative genomics was supervised by Dr. Kuljeet Singh Sandhu in the Genome Biology Lab. In 2024 I was a Talent+ research intern with Dr. Clyde Francks in the Imaging Genomics group of the Language and Genetics Department at the Max Planck Institute for Psycholinguistics, Nijmegen. Since February 2026 I have been working independently, in collaboration with Dr. Verma, on handedness and the lateralization of visuospatial attention.

Research

Handedness and the lateralization of visuospatial attention

Current

Independent researcher, with Dr. Ark Verma (IIT Kanpur) · Feb. 2026 – present

A four-experiment divided visual half-field study (orientation and spatial frequency, global–local form, faces, words) in 104 participants (102 analysed) recruited across IIT Kanpur and NIT Calicut, reported preliminarily at CogSci 2023 and now carried through to completion. Hierarchical Bayesian drift-diffusion modelling of 23,273 trials (brms/Stan) localises the visual-field × handedness interaction to nondecision time, with robustness checked by permutation, specification-curve, equivalence and cross-site heterogeneity analyses.

Handedness continuum and lateralized cognitive functions

2019 – 2021

Master's thesis, IIT Kanpur · Supervisor: Dr. Ark Verma

Three divided visual half-field experiments — tool recognition, action-word processing and symmetry recognition — in 39 participants sampled across the full handedness continuum, testing whether the degree of handedness predicts hemispheric lateralization. Handedness affected both the degree and the direction of lateralization in tool and symmetry processing, but only the degree in action-word processing, suggesting that handedness has differential effects on the lateralization of low- and high-level processes.

Genomic rearrangement and cancer resistance in rodents

2017 – 2018

BS-MS thesis, IISER Mohali · Supervisor: Dr. Kuljeet Singh Sandhu

Comparative genomic analysis across mammals uncovered a large rearrangement in the naked mole-rat genome that splits a tumour-suppressor gene cluster relative to humans. The convergent rearrangement is confined to two rodent suborders that also show reduced oncogenesis, bearing on the exceptional cancer resistance of those species.
