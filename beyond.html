Beyond research

Away from academia

Between research positions I worked outside the university. I co-founded a small company, and spent time in places built around community and sustainability. I like working with my hands to build things and keeping my body moving, and these years gave me plenty of both: I ran daily operations for Farmer’s Share, an organic farming collective, lived as a community member at Urvi Earth Centre, a space for experimenting with the human ego and consciousness, and volunteered on food-forest and vernacular-architecture projects at Dhaanyam.

  • 2026
    Dhaanyam Food Forest, Attappadi — volunteer, working on tribal architecture and sustainability.
  • 2025
    Urvi Earth Centre, Munnar — community member; participated in community and meditation activities, and built mud houses using local, earthen materials.
  • 2024
    Farmer's Share Permaculture and Craft Learning Center, Shornur — ran daily operations for an organic farming collective and led immersive educational camps on sustainable living and permaculture.
  • 2022 – 2024
    Growers Haven LLP, Pollachi — founding partner; ran operations end to end, managed people and suppliers, and delivered against deadlines.

The inner world

Looking back, that time away from academia has been the most valuable time I have had. It let me explore the mind and life outside an academic research setting, in a more dialectical way, where life and mind keep acting on each other. It also gave me room to explore mindfulness practices and alternative spaces. I keep up a daily mindfulness practice, with occasional residential Vipassana and Satipaṭṭhāna courses, and I have been exploring Dzogchen on my own. Whoever you are, I highly recommend exploring your own inner world.

“Most persons are so absorbed in the contemplation of the outside world that they are wholly oblivious to what is passing on within themselves.”

— Nikola Tesla, My Inventions, 1919

“…thought has explored the universe, gone as far as Venus, Saturn. … We have never been able to fathom the enormous energy that lies in the mind.”

— J. Krishnamurti, 4th Public Talk, Colombo, 1980
